## How can you keep brokerage fees low in Musashikosugi?

**In short: choose an agency that publishes its fee structure before you start looking at properties.** If the fee is fixed from the start, such as “half a month’s rent plus tax” for rentals or “half the legal maximum” for sales, there is nothing to negotiate property by property.

Rents and sale prices in Musashikosugi are on the high side, so a difference in the fee *rate* turns into a difference of tens of thousands to over a million yen. This article explains how the legal caps work, what the actual difference is, and what to check when an agency is cheap.

## What is the legal maximum brokerage fee?

**In short: for rentals, one month’s rent plus consumption tax; for sales above ¥4 million, 3% of the sale price plus ¥60,000 plus tax.** These caps come from Japan’s Real Estate Brokerage Act (宅地建物取引業法), and no agency may charge more.

- **Rentals**: the cap applies to the total received from landlord and tenant combined. The tenant’s share is in principle half a month’s rent plus tax, and the agency may receive up to one month plus tax from the tenant only with the tenant’s consent. How that consent is obtained is a point to confirm before you sign.
- **Sales**: for prices above ¥4 million, the quick formula for the cap is 3% of the sale price + ¥60,000 + tax.
- **Low-priced properties**: since July 2024, a special rule allows a cap of up to ¥300,000 plus tax for properties priced at ¥8 million or less. The conditions should be confirmed case by case, and this rarely applies to condominiums in Musashikosugi.

The important point is that **these are maximums, not list prices**. There is no legal minimum, so each agency sets its own fee.

## What should I check when an agency is cheap?

**In short: three things. Which properties it can handle, what service it provides, and whether there are fees other than the brokerage fee.** A low fee can mean the agency has cut unnecessary steps, or it can mean the money is collected under another name.

1. **Which properties it can handle**: most listings are shared among agencies through the industry database (REINS) and portal sites, so in principle you can apply through any agency. A few properties are handled exclusively by one agency. The safest approach is to find the property first and then ask, “Can you handle this one?”
2. **Scope of service**: accompanied viewings, negotiating conditions, explaining the contract documents, and coordinating the handover vary by agency. Whether everything can be done online without visiting an office is another point to check. For non-Japanese clients, ask whether the agency will explain the documents in English.
3. **Other fees**: for rentals, watch for “administrative fees”, “document fees” or “disinfection fees”; for sales, “loan processing fees”. Compare the **total on the written estimate**, not just the rate.

## What are the concrete steps?

**In short: pick the agency first, then find the property, then compare total estimates.**

1. **Find an agency with a published fee structure**: shortlist agencies that state their rates on their website.
2. **Search through that agency, or bring a property you found elsewhere**: properties found on portal sites can usually be handled too. Ask to be sure.
3. **Compare written estimates by total**: check for fees under other names in addition to the brokerage fee.
4. **Confirm again at the explanation of important matters**: the fee amount and payment timing are stated in the contract documents. In Japan the Japanese documents are the legally binding ones; ask for an English translation for reference and an explanation in English.
